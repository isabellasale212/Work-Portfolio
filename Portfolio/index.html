Dissertation · BSc Research Project · Dr Adam Brazil

Does jump-test power transfer to change-of-direction braking?

Force-plate study of 20 athletes — do jump qualities predict deceleration and 505 change-of-direction speed?

n = 20Force plates · 2000 HzRM-ANOVA
r = −0.55CMJ peak RFD ↔ 505 time (p = 0.014)

Finding: jump power is task-specific and doesn’t transfer — deceleration needs its own force-plate metrics.

method

Eccentric rate of force development from countermovement, drop and broad jumps vs the braking phase of a 505 task; Pearson/Spearman correlations, t-tests and RM-ANOVA (η²p = 0.77). Marker feedback: “a very good introduction… a well-written abstract… methods that would allow for replication.”

Lab report · Applied Sport Biomechanics · 2026

Knee kinematics of the instep football kick: dominant vs non-dominant leg

A 3D kinematic analysis of how knee-joint variables differ between legs during an instep kick, and how strongly they relate to ball speed.

3D kinematicsBilateralBall speed

Skills shown: motion-capture data processing, joint-angle computation and correlation analysis.

method

3D marker data reduced to knee flexion/extension angles across the kicking action, compared bilaterally and correlated against measured ball speed — quantitative biomechanics on real captured data.
